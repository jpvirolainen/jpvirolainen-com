I've built several startups around mobile marketing. The whole game was the customer model: understand the customer well enough to influence what they want next.

Personal AI changes that dynamic, and this time I'm watching it as the consumer.

My agent can know far more about me than any brand ever should: my calendar, my past purchases, my constraints, and whatever else I choose to share with it. None of that has to leak back to a marketer.

A brand can still make an offer, and it still has good reasons to target and time that offer intelligently. The difference is that my agent decides how the offer compares against alternatives, and whether I get interrupted at all.

That flips who holds the better customer model. Every brand only sees its own slice of me, filtered through what it wants to sell. My agent sees the broader picture and operates on the preferences I gave it.

This isn't far off. People already ask AI tools to compare products and strip away marketing claims. A personal agent will do the same continuously without being asked.

Marketers will dislike losing that control, and I understand why; I spent years on their side of the table. Consumers won't miss being targeted, and if enough of them put an agent between themselves and outbound marketing, brands don't get a vote.

Marketing doesn't disappear. Brands still need to be known, trusted, and preferred. Close to the purchase decision, though, the buyer finally has something representing their side too.

One risk is the business model behind the agent itself. If the company running your personal agent also runs an advertising business, it decides whose interests the agent actually serves. If it gets paid by advertisers, the customer model didn't move to the customer; it changed hands again.
