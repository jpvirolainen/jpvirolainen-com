For years, businesses have been told to build standalone apps for everything: customer support, bookings, loyalty programs, order tracking. The result is severe app fatigue. Convincing someone to download yet another single-purpose app, create an account, and remember a password is a losing battle.

A legitimate question is how many of those interactions actually need a dedicated app once rich messaging and conversational AI are combined.

As RCS gains broad device traction after a long, uneven rollout, business messaging is no longer limited to plain text. Companies can send interactive messages with media, structured options, and action buttons inside the native messaging client users already open dozens of times a day.

That makes RCS very different from a mobile website trapped inside a chat window. A web page waits for the customer to search, find a menu, and log in. An AI-driven messaging thread can reach out proactively at the right moment, keep context persistent over time, and adapt its responses based on the user's actual request.

Instead of asking a traveler to open an airline app, hunt through menus, or re-authenticate on a mobile browser, a messaging thread can handle rebooking, seat changes, or boarding updates inside one continuous conversation.

This goes well beyond static notification alerts or rigid decision-tree chatbots. It puts context-aware execution inside channels that already have universal reach.

For communications platform providers, the opportunity is straightforward. Platforms that stay confined to raw message delivery compete on commodity pricing. The ones that combine reliable delivery with conversation state, verification, and AI orchestration turn messaging itself into the customer interface.

Native apps aren't disappearing for complex, high-frequency tools. Still, intelligent messaging will steadily absorb a large share of everyday business interactions that never justified an app download in the first place.
